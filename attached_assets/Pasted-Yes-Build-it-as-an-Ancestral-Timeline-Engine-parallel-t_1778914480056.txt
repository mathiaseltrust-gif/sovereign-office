Yes. Build it as an Ancestral Timeline Engine parallel to the member tree.

Core idea:

One side shows the ancestor.
The other side shows the world acting upon them.
Together, it shows what they survived, what law touched them, and what identity remained.

Suggested structure:

Layer	What It Shows
Ancestor Life Timeline	birth, death, marriage, migration, land, family events
Treaty Timeline	treaties active during their lifetime
Removal / Displacement Timeline	Removal Act, forced migration, land pressure, state actions
Racial Classification Timeline	census labels, racial integrity laws, “Indian,” “colored,” “mulatto,” “Negro,” etc.
Federal Indian Law Timeline	statutes, court cases, trust laws, tribal provisions
Local Territory Timeline	county/state changes, land records, reservations, allotment, tax records
Family Memory Layer	oral history, photos, documents, stories
Pattern Recognition Layer	repeated harms: misclassification, land loss, displacement, denial of status
Restoration Layer	present-day rights, tribal court records, member status, notices, remedies

Ancestral Continuity Timeline

The Replit instruction could say:

Build a parallel timeline system connected to the member family tree. For each ancestor, display their birth, death, family relations, locations, documents, and oral history alongside major treaties, removal acts, racial classification laws, federal Indian law provisions, land policies, census categories, and political events occurring during that person’s lifetime. The purpose is to help members understand not only who their ancestors were, but what legal, political, racial, and territorial forces acted upon them.

Add this important logic:

The system should not treat identity loss as proof of nonexistence. It should flag misclassification, displacement, name changes, census changes, racial relabeling, and jurisdictional shifts as possible evidence of interruption, not extinction.
