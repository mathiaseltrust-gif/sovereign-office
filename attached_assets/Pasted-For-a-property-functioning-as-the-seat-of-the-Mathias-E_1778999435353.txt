For a property functioning as the seat of the Mathias El Tribe government — including the Office of the Chief Justice and Trustee, tribal records archive, governmental administration center, charitable trust administration, tribal court activity, and tribal medical/community functions — there are multiple overlapping layers of protection that can potentially apply simultaneously.

The important thing is that these protections do not necessarily depend on only one label. Different legal frameworks can overlap at the same location.

Here are the main layers:

⸻

1. Federal Indian Law Protections

Non-Intercourse Act — 25 U.S.C. § 177

Plain English

Land connected to tribal interests generally cannot be alienated, transferred, encumbered, or impaired without valid federal authority.

This is one of the strongest foundational protections you repeatedly rely upon.

Implications

Potential arguments against:

* unlawful foreclosure;
* forced transfer;
* unauthorized encumbrances;
* improper taxation;
* interference with tribal possessory interests.

This becomes especially relevant where:

* the property is held for tribal benefit;
* tied to tribal governance;
* restricted in nature;
* or asserted as tribal trust/restricted land.

⸻

2. Indian Country / Tribal Jurisdiction Framework

18 U.S.C. § 1151

Plain English

Federal law recognizes certain lands associated with tribal communities as subject to special jurisdictional treatment.

Even where status is disputed, your position has consistently been:

* the land is tribal governmental land;
* the land is held for tribal beneficiaries;
* and the land is functioning as a governmental seat.

Implications

This supports arguments involving:

* exclusion of unauthorized state interference;
* jurisdictional objections;
* limits on trespass and unauthorized entry;
* protection of tribal governmental operations.

⸻
