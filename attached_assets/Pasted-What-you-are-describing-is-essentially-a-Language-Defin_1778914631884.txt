What you are describing is essentially a:

Language, Definition, and Jurisdiction Literacy System

And honestly, that may become one of the most important parts of SDU because most people are not defeated by force first — they are defeated by definitions they never learned to examine.

Not “word games” in a manipulative sense alone, but:

* jurisdiction through language,
* classification through terminology,
* authority through framing,
* and limitation through administrative narrowing.

Your instinct is correct that this should probably begin with a foundational explanation block before definitions appear. Otherwise people may read definitions mechanically without understanding why definitions matter.

A strong framing concept for SDU could be:

⸻

Why Definitions Matter

Words determine:

* jurisdiction,
* eligibility,
* classification,
* authority,
* protection,
* obligation,
* and access to remedies.

A single change in definition can alter:

* whether a people are protected,
* whether land is treated as protected,
* whether benefits attach,
* whether jurisdiction applies,
* or whether a community is treated as continuing or nonexistent.

Many legal conflicts are not fought openly through force first, but through interpretation, classification, and administrative narrowing.

For this reason, understanding definitions is part of self-determination, legal literacy, and historical comprehension.

Suggested Structure

1. Foundational Meaning Layer

Start with:

* ordinary meaning,
* historical meaning,
* dictionary meaning,
* treaty-era meaning.

Example sources:

* Noah Webster’s American Dictionary of the English Language
* Black’s Law Dictionary
* treaty terminology
* constitutional-era usage

This is important because it shows:

language before modern administrative narrowing.

⸻

2. Federal Statutory Definition Layer

Then show:

Provision	Definition Scope
25 U.S.C. § 5304(e)	broad tribal definition
25 U.S.C. § 1603	broad Indian healthcare definitions
Snyder Act	“Indians throughout the United States”
ICWA	tribal child/family definitions
Medicaid AI/AN provisions	eligibility protections
ICRA	tribal governance definitions

This becomes:

“Congressional operational definitions.”

⸻

3. Administrative Definition Layer

Then distinguish:

Type	Purpose
Congressional statute	law enacted by Congress
Agency regulation	implementation mechanism
Administrative list	recordkeeping / funding administration
Internal guidance	agency interpretation

This is where you can carefully explain:

* administrative systems are not always identical to the full scope of federal Indian law,
* and agencies sometimes use narrower operational criteria for specific programs.

That framing is more durable and educational than simply saying:

“the list is fake.”

Instead:

“Administrative lists serve administrative purposes and do not necessarily exhaust the full scope of federal Indian statutory language, historical continuity, treaty history, or Indigenous identity.”

That distinction is extremely important for credibility.

⸻

4. Parallel Definition Comparison Tool

This part would be incredibly powerful in SDU.

Example UI:

Word	Common Understanding	Legal Meaning	Administrative Meaning	Historical Meaning
Indian	race label	political/legal classification	eligibility category	Indigenous nationhood
Tribe	social group	self-governing political community	recognized entity	kinship nation
Recognition	acknowledgment	confirmation of relationship	agency process	continued existence
Jurisdiction	control	lawful authority	agency territory	relational governance

This teaches:

“Words can operate differently depending on who is defining them.”

⸻

5. Pattern Recognition Engine

Your AI Companion could help members identify:

* narrowing language,
* circular definitions,
* hidden assumptions,
* administrative substitution,
* undefined terms,
* jurisdictional presumptions,
* and contradictory definitions across agencies.

Example:

“This document uses ‘federally recognized tribe’ without defining whether it refers to:

1. the Part 83 administrative process,
2. statutory tribal eligibility,
3. treaty relationship,
4. political community,
    or 5. program administration.”

That is powerful educationally.

⸻

6. A Very Important Framing Principle

I would avoid framing this as:

“Everything was a conspiracy through words.”

Instead frame it as:

“Language systems shape perception, classification, and legal outcomes. Understanding definitions allows individuals and communities to better understand history, governance, and the operation of law.”

That keeps SDU grounded, educational, and intellectually serious.

⸻

7. Your Deeper SDU Insight

What you are really teaching is:

* the difference between:
    * identity,
    * classification,
    * eligibility,
    * administration,
    * and existence.

That distinction alone can transform how people understand themselves and their history.
