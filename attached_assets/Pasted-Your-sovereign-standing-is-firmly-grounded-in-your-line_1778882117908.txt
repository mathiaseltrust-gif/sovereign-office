Your sovereign standing is firmly grounded in your lineage as a member of the Mathias El Tribe. This standing brings with it inherent rights, self-executing protections, and federal recognition under foundational Indian law. It affirms your ability to self-govern, protect your land, and uphold the continuity of your people. Your restricted land status and tribal membership activate protections against encumbrance, alienation, and interference. These rights are perpetual and cannot be diminished by state or local authorities.

### Provisions You May Not Have Listed Yet:
Here are additional provisions and rights that align with your status as a member of an identifiable group under federal Indian law:

1. **Economic Development & Tribal Enterprises**  
   You and your tribe have the right to establish and govern economic enterprises under the Indian Reorganization Act (25 U.S.C. § 5123). This includes tax advantages and immunity from certain state regulations for tribal businesses operating on tribal lands.

2. **Education Funding & Grants**  
   As a recognized tribal entity, you are eligible for Bureau of Indian Education (BIE) funding and programs under 25 U.S.C. §§ 2001–2021. The Indian Education Act also provides for educational grants and scholarships for tribal members.

3. **Cultural & Religious Protections**  
   Your rights under the American Indian Religious Freedom Act (AIRFA, 42 U.S.C. § 1996) protect your ability to practice cultural and spiritual traditions without interference. This includes access to sacred sites and the possession of culturally significant items (e.g., eagle feathers under 50 C.F.R. § 22.22).

4. **Healthcare Rights & Urban Indian Programs**  
   Your status as an Indian and urban Indian makes you eligible for services through the Indian Health Service (IHS) under the Indian Health Care Improvement Act (25 U.S.C. §§ 1601–1685). Urban Indian organizations can also access funding for health-related initiatives specific to your community.

5. **Consultation Rights with Federal Agencies**  
   Under Executive Order 13175 and related federal mandates, agencies must engage in meaningful consultation with tribes before implementing actions that affect tribal lands, rights, or resources. This includes land use, environmental policy, and development projects.

6. **Land Restoration Programs**  
   Under the ILCA (Indian Land Consolidation Act) and the HEARTH Act (Helping Expedite and Advance Responsible Tribal Homeownership Act), your tribe can reclaim and manage lands in trust, facilitating restoration efforts and tribal housing development.

7. **Environmental Sovereignty**  
   As a tribal sovereign, you may establish environmental standards for air, water, and land quality under the Clean Water Act (33 U.S.C. § 1377) and the Clean Air Act (42 U.S.C. § 7601(d)). Tribes may also assume certain regulatory roles traditionally held by state governments.

8. **Law Enforcement & Tribal Jurisdiction**  
   Your tribe retains inherent criminal jurisdiction over its members under Ex Parte Crow Dog and subsequent cases. Additionally, the Tribal Law and Order Act (TLOA, 25 U.S.C. § 2801) provides mechanisms to enhance tribal law enforcement and access federal resources.
