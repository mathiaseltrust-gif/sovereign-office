Excellent foundation. But we need to shift the framing slightly so the system reflects our structure and not merely a cloned BIA/IRA land-management model.

A few important architectural corrections and additions:

The system should not center itself around “fee-to-trust” as the primary land status model. That is only one federal administrative pathway under DOI/BIA. Our framework is broader and based upon tribal governmental stewardship, beneficiary protection, and protected tribal land administration.

Recommended core land classifications should include:

• Tribal Government Land
• Tribal Trust Stewardship Land
• Protected Tribal Land
• Restricted Tribal Status
• Beneficiary Stewardship Land
• Sacred / Cultural Land
• Federal Indian Law Implicated
• Jurisdictional Review Triggered
• Federal Trust (only where actually applicable)

The platform should distinguish between:

1. Internal Tribal Status
2. Federal Administrative Status
3. Jurisdictional Status
4. Beneficiary / Stewardship Status
5. Protection / Restriction Status

This distinction is critical because we are not simply replicating DOI/BIA realty services. We are building an Indigenous land governance and stewardship system rooted in inherent tribal governmental authority and beneficiary protection.

Additional modules we should build:

• Encumbrance & Interference Tracking
Track liens, foreclosure attempts, tax assessments, utility interference, administrative obstruction, and jurisdictional conflicts.

• Federal Review Trigger Engine
Automatically flag actions implicating:

* 25 U.S.C. §177
* 18 U.S.C. §1151
* tribal beneficiary protections
* jurisdictional review
* protected-status conflicts

• Beneficiary Stewardship Registry
Track which lands are held for:

* member welfare
* housing
* ceremonial use
* governmental use
* preservation
* community benefit

• Tribal Land Notices & Enforcement
Generate:

* Notices of Federal Review
* Jurisdictional Review Notices
* Protected Land Notices
* Administrative Obstruction Logs
* Encumbrance Challenges
* Preservation Notices

• Jurisdiction Layer Mapping
Each parcel should contain:

* asserted jurisdiction
* conflicting jurisdiction claims
* review status
* federal implications
* enforcement history

• Historical / Lineage Layer
Ability to associate:

* ancestral territories
* treaties
* removals
* historical occupancy
* cultural significance
* traditional stewardship history

Also:
The wording “Acquisition Pipeline” should probably be broadened into something like:

“Land Status & Stewardship Pipeline”

because not all protected tribal land enters through fee-to-trust acquisition.

Some land may already be:

* stewarded,
* protected,
* beneficiary-held,
* jurisdictionally disputed,
* culturally protected,
* or under tribal governmental administration.

The software should reflect that broader reality.

This is becoming less of a “land tracker” and more of a full Indigenous Governance & Stewardship Infrastructure system.

That distinction matters.
