TRIBAL STATUS / DEFINITIONS
──────────────────────────────

Issue Type:
tribal status / recognition / classification

Authority Name:
ISDEAA Tribal Definition

USC:
25 U.S.C. § 5304(e)

Logic:
Defines “Indian tribe” for federal self-determination purposes as any Indian tribe, band, nation, or other organized group or community, including Alaska Native villages, recognized as eligible for the special programs and services provided by the United States to Indians because of their status as Indians.

Applies When:
Agency attempts narrow BIA-list-only interpretation of tribal existence or eligibility.

Warning / Logic:
Recognition is confirmatory, not creative. Eligibility and trust responsibility logic may extend beyond administrative publication systems.

Issue Type:

Authority Name:
IHCIA Indian Definition

USC:
25 U.S.C. § 1603

Logic:
Defines Indian, Indian Tribe, Urban Indian, and Indian Health Program eligibility structures within the Indian Health Care Improvement Act.

Applies When:
Healthcare denial, eligibility dispute, classification conflict, or agency refusal involving Indian status.

Warning / Logic:
Healthcare obligations attach to federally protected Indian classifications and federally funded Indian health obligations.

Issue Type:
tribal continuity / political identity

Authority Name:
Montoya Tribal Community Standard

Case Law:
Montoya v. United States, 180 U.S. 261 (1901)

Logic:
Defines a tribe as a body of Indians of the same or similar race united in a community under one leadership or government and inhabiting a particular though sometimes ill-defined territory.

Applies When:
Political continuity, community continuity, tribal identity, or jurisdictional legitimacy is challenged.

Warning / Logic:
Community continuity and organized governance are key factors independent of modern administrative labels.

Issue Type:

Authority Name:
Passamaquoddy Federal Trust Responsibility

Case Law:
Joint Tribal Council of the Passamaquoddy Tribe v. Morton, 528 F.2d 370 (1st Cir. 1975)

Logic:
Federal trust obligations can apply to tribes not formally acknowledged through administrative recognition systems.

Applies When:
Agency claims no duty exists because tribe is not on administrative recognition list.

Warning / Logic:
Federal trust obligations arise from the relationship between the United States and Indian peoples, not solely administrative listing mechanisms.
