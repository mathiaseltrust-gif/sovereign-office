Build an interactive web application called “Urban Indian Continuity Atlas.”

Purpose:
This application must show how laws, public policies, federal/state classifications, urbanization, public schools, health systems, census records, housing policy, labor migration, and administrative reclassification affected Indigenous identity, family continuity, community memory, and modern urban Indian life.

This is not primarily a boarding school project. Boarding schools may appear only when relevant to a broader policy era, source collection, or identity-disruption pattern. The main focus is urban Indians, reclassified families, public-school assimilation, migration, city records, health access, and continuity despite government classification.

Core Concept:
The app is a historical continuity and impact engine for urban Indians. It must combine:
1. timeline
2. map
3. policy filters
4. identity/reclassification filters
5. family/ancestor overlays
6. source archive
7. modern impact notes

Primary Question:
How did federal, state, county, school, health, census, and urban policies affect Indigenous identity and continuity across generations?

Do not frame Indigenous people as disappearing.
Separate actual identity from government classification.
Show continuity despite reclassification.

Urban Indian Focus Areas:
The app must track:
- movement from tribal/rural lands into cities
- public school assimilation
- loss or alteration of Indian identity in records
- census racial reclassification
- state and county record distortion
- health eligibility barriers
- housing displacement
- employment/labor migration
- loss of community record continuity
- continued Indigenous identity despite paperwork changes
