# IN THE MATHIAS EL TRIBE SUPREME COURT

**In re Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001**  
**Parcel Reference No. 76-24-16-201-019**  
**Case No. MET-SC-SM-2026-001**

# OPINION OF THE COURT, DECLARATORY JUDGMENT, AND FINAL JUDGMENT

## SYLLABUS

This matter comes before the Mathias El Tribe Supreme Court for determination of the legal effect of the conveyances, recorded instruments, and authenticated materials contained in the judicial record concerning Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001, commonly referred to as the Bell Road matter.

The Court does not create a new property interest, enlarge the terms of a conveyance, or decide questions unnecessary to disposition of the controversy. Its function is to identify the instruments properly before it, determine the law governing their interpretation, and declare the legal consequences established by that law and record.

The Court holds that the legal effect of a conveyance must be determined from the instrument, the authority under which it was executed, and the law governing its operation. Recording preserves and gives notice of an instrument according to applicable law; recording alone does not supply terms that the instrument does not contain. Likewise, governmental or private action cannot lawfully enlarge, diminish, or disregard an instrument except through authority traceable to governing law.

The instruments concerning MET-TL-SM-001 must therefore be construed together to the extent that they concern the same parcel, transaction, trust relationship, or governmental purpose. Each operative provision must be given effect where reasonably possible, and no instrument may be treated as legally ineffective merely because a later actor disputes its stated purpose or consequences.

This Opinion resolves only the controversy and instruments presented in the authenticated record. It does not adjudicate the rights of persons who were not properly before the Court, and it does not determine issues not necessary to the judgment entered herein.

## OPINION OF THE COURT

### I. Nature of the Proceeding

This proceeding concerns the judicial construction of conveyances and recorded instruments affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001, identified in the record by Parcel Reference No. 76-24-16-201-019.

The Court is asked to determine the legal operation of those instruments and to resolve uncertainty concerning the rights, interests, duties, and obligations arising from them. The Court exercises only the authority necessary to decide that controversy and reserves all questions not required for disposition.

### II. Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction is neither presumed nor created by judicial declaration. It must arise from the governing law that authorizes the Court to hear and determine the controversy presented.

The authenticated record presents instruments concerning land designated by the Mathias El Tribe as MET-TL-SM-001 and a concrete dispute concerning the interpretation and legal consequences of those instruments. The matter is therefore one of judicial construction rather than abstract advice.

The Court concludes that it possesses jurisdiction, under the law governing this Court and the proceeding, to interpret the instruments properly presented, determine their legal effect as between the interests properly before it, and enter declaratory and final relief within that authority. This judgment extends no further than that jurisdiction permits.

### III. Judicial Authority and Function

The authority exercised by this Court is not self-created and does not arise from assertion alone. Every exercise of judicial power must be traceable to a lawful source and remain within the limits of that source.

The Court's responsibility is to identify, interpret, and apply governing law to facts established by the authenticated record. It does not legislate, administer policy, create title by declaration, or substitute judicial preference for the terms of an instrument or the law controlling it.

The Court therefore applies the following method:

1. identify the instruments properly contained in the judicial record;
2. identify the law governing those instruments and this proceeding;
3. determine the relevant facts established by authenticated materials;
4. construe related instruments together where the law permits;
5. declare only the legal consequences supported by the instruments and governing law; and
6. enter relief no broader than necessary to resolve the controversy.

### IV. Questions Presented

The questions necessary to disposition are:

1. Which conveyances and recorded instruments concerning MET-TL-SM-001 are properly before the Court?
2. What law governs their interpretation and legal operation?
3. What legal consequences arise from those instruments when construed according to that law?
4. What declaratory and final relief is necessary to resolve the controversy presented by the authenticated record?

All other questions are reserved.

### V. Standard and Principles of Construction

Questions concerning the interpretation and legal effect of written conveyances are determined independently by the Court under the law applicable to those instruments.

The Court begins with the text of each instrument. It considers the instrument as a whole, the lawful authority under which it was executed, its relationship to other instruments in the same transaction or chain of conveyance, and the authenticated record properly before the Court.

Where text is clear, the Court gives effect to its ordinary legal meaning. Where an ambiguity material to the controversy exists, the Court construes the instrument to give reasonable effect to the whole, harmonize related provisions where possible, and avoid rendering operative language meaningless.

Recording is evidence of an instrument and may provide notice or priority according to applicable law. Recording does not, standing alone, create a substantive interest absent a valid instrument or other lawful source. Conversely, the legal operation of a valid instrument is not defeated merely by a later characterization unsupported by governing law.

### VI. Findings of Fact

Based solely on the authenticated materials contained in the judicial record, the Court finds:

1. The property at issue is identified in this proceeding as Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001 and Parcel Reference No. 76-24-16-201-019.
2. The judicial record contains conveyances and related recorded instruments concerning that property.
3. Those instruments were presented to the Court for interpretation as related parts of the Bell Road matter.
4. The instruments concern the same identified property and include asserted legal purposes, relationships, and consequences that must be determined from their text and governing law.
5. A present controversy exists concerning the legal effect to be accorded those instruments.
6. No finding in this Opinion rests upon materials outside the authenticated judicial record.
7. The Court makes no finding concerning an interest, person, instrument, or transaction not properly presented in this proceeding.

### VII. Conclusions of Law

#### Conclusion No. 1 — Governing Law Controls

The legal effect of the instruments concerning MET-TL-SM-001 must be determined under the law governing their execution, acknowledgment, delivery, recording, interpretation, and enforcement. Practice, custom, convenience, or unilateral assertion cannot substitute for governing law.

#### Conclusion No. 2 — The Court Declares; It Does Not Create

The Court neither creates a property interest nor extinguishes an existing interest by description alone. It declares the consequences that governing law assigns to the instruments and facts established by the record.

#### Conclusion No. 3 — Related Instruments Must Be Construed Together

To the extent the instruments concern the same property, transaction, trust relationship, or governmental purpose, they must be read together where governing law permits. Each operative provision must be given effect whenever reasonably possible.

#### Conclusion No. 4 — Authority Must Be Demonstrated

Any governmental or private act asserted to alter, defeat, enlarge, or diminish rights established by the instruments must be supported by an identifiable source of lawful authority. An act inconsistent with governing law has no effect to the extent of that inconsistency.

#### Conclusion No. 5 — Authentication and Recording Do Not Replace Substantive Validity

Execution, acknowledgment, authentication, and recording are legally significant according to the law applicable to each act. They do not cure a substantively invalid instrument, but neither may a facially operative instrument be disregarded without a lawful ground established under the same governing law.

#### Conclusion No. 6 — Declaratory Relief Is Appropriate but Limited

Because a present controversy exists concerning the legal effect of instruments properly before the Court, declaratory relief is appropriate. Such relief must remain confined to the instruments, interests, and issues actually adjudicated.

#### Conclusion No. 7 — Unnecessary Questions Remain Reserved

The Court declines to determine title, jurisdictional, enforcement, or governmental questions not necessary to interpret the instruments and resolve the controversy presented by the authenticated record.

### VIII. Analysis and Holding

The dispute is neither hypothetical nor advisory. It concerns what legal consequences follow from instruments in the authenticated record affecting MET-TL-SM-001.

A conveyance derives its legal operation from its text and governing law. Recording may preserve the instrument, establish notice, or affect priority, but the recorded status of a document does not permit the Court to add terms that the document does not contain. The inverse is also true: a later actor cannot nullify or recast an operative instrument merely by refusing to recognize its expressed terms. A claimed power to alter an instrument must be traced to law.

The instruments in this record concern the same identified property and were presented as parts of a related course of conveyance and designation. They must therefore be examined together to the extent allowed by governing law. That construction does not merge distinct legal acts or erase separate requirements. It permits the Court to understand each instrument in its proper relationship to the others while preserving the independent legal significance of each document.

The Court rejects any rule under which an authenticated and recorded instrument may be declared ineffective solely because an administrative body, private party, or later governmental actor assigns it a different characterization. The validity and effect of the instrument must instead be tested under the law governing the interest asserted, the authority of the person or entity executing it, and any conditions applicable to delivery, acceptance, recording, priority, or enforcement.

On the record presented, the Court finds no lawful basis to treat the instruments as nonexistent or devoid of all legal effect. The Court therefore recognizes each instrument to the extent—and only to the extent—that its text and governing law make it operative. Nothing in this holding converts a designation, trust declaration, deed, recording, or judicial characterization into a greater interest than the law permits.

Accordingly, the Court holds that:

1. the instruments properly contained in the authenticated record are legally cognizable and must be evaluated rather than ignored;
2. related instruments must be construed together without collapsing their distinct legal functions;
3. their effect is controlled by their text and governing law, not by unsupported later characterization;
4. any person or governmental body asserting authority to alter or defeat their legal operation must identify lawful authority for that action; and
5. the judgment binds only the matters and interests properly adjudicated in this proceeding.

## DECLARATORY JUDGMENT

For the reasons stated above, the Court declares:

### Declaration No. 1 — Instruments Before the Court

The conveyances and recorded instruments admitted into the authenticated judicial record concerning MET-TL-SM-001 are the controlling documentary sources for determining the rights, interests, duties, and obligations adjudicated in this proceeding.

### Declaration No. 2 — Governing Law and Text

Each instrument shall be given the legal effect assigned by its text and the law governing its execution, delivery, acknowledgment, authentication, recording, interpretation, and enforcement.

### Declaration No. 3 — Unified but Distinct Construction

Related instruments shall be construed together where legally appropriate, while preserving the distinct legal function, requirements, and effect of each instrument.

### Declaration No. 4 — No Alteration Without Lawful Authority

No administrative practice, private action, governmental assumption, or later characterization may enlarge, diminish, or defeat the legal operation of an instrument absent authority supplied by governing law.

### Declaration No. 5 — Limits of the Declaration

This Judgment does not create title, adjudicate the rights of persons not properly before the Court, determine the priority of an instrument not adjudicated in this proceeding, or resolve questions reserved by the Court. It declares only the legal principles and consequences necessarily established by the authenticated record and governing law.

### Declaration No. 6 — Continuing Effect

These declarations remain effective within the jurisdiction of this Court unless modified, vacated, stayed, or superseded through a lawful proceeding by a tribunal possessing authority to do so.

## ORDER AND DECREE

It is ORDERED, ADJUDGED, AND DECREED:

1. **Adoption.** This Opinion, its Findings of Fact, Conclusions of Law, Analysis and Holding, and Declaratory Judgment are adopted as the decision of the Court.
2. **Judicial Construction.** The instruments concerning MET-TL-SM-001 shall be understood and administered consistently with this Opinion and the governing law applicable to each instrument.
3. **Certified Copies.** The Clerk may prepare and certify true and correct copies for service, publication, recording, or another lawful purpose. Certification authenticates the Court's judgment; it does not enlarge the substantive effect of any underlying instrument.
4. **Preservation of Record.** The Clerk shall preserve the authenticated record, admitted exhibits, conveyances, recorded instruments, and this Judgment as permanent court records.
5. **Continuing Jurisdiction.** The Court retains jurisdiction only to interpret, implement, correct clerical errors in, and enforce this Judgment within the limits of governing law. This reservation does not reopen matters finally adjudicated.
6. **Other Relief.** Relief not expressly granted is denied without prejudice to a later proceeding where governing law permits and the matter is properly presented.
