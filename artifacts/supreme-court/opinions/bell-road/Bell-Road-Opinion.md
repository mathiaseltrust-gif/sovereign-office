OPINION OF THE COURT, DECLARATORY JUDGMENT, AND FINAL JUDGMENT
 
SYLLABUS
This matter comes before the Mathias El Tribe Supreme Court for determination of the legal effect of the governing conveyances, recorded instruments, and authenticated record affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001, commonly referred to throughout these proceedings as the Bell Road matter.
The controversy does not require the Court to create new law, enlarge existing authority, or decide issues unnecessary to the disposition of the case. Rather, the judicial function is limited to determining the legal effect of the governing instruments according to the law from which they derive their authority.
Accordingly, the Court examines the authenticated record, identifies the governing law applicable to the recorded conveyances, determines the legal effect of those instruments, declares the respective rights and interests arising therefrom, and enters final judgment consistent with those determinations.
The Court further holds that governmental authority is not presumed, but must be demonstrated by reference to the governing law from which it is claimed to arise. Likewise, the legal effect of an authenticated conveyance cannot be enlarged, diminished, or disregarded except according to governing law.
This Opinion constitutes the Court's final judicial determination concerning the issues presented by the record before it.
 
OPINION OF THE COURT
I. Caption
This proceeding concerns the judicial interpretation of the governing conveyances and recorded instruments affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001.
The matter is before the Mathias El Tribe Supreme Court pursuant to its judicial authority to determine controversies involving the interpretation, construction, legal effect, implementation, and enforcement of governing instruments properly presented to the Court.
The Court exercises only the authority necessary to decide the controversy presented by the authenticated record and reserves all questions not required for disposition of this matter.
 
II. Jurisdiction
Jurisdiction is neither presumed nor created by judicial declaration. It must arise from the governing law that authorizes the Court to hear and determine the controversy presented.
Before addressing the merits, the Court must therefore determine that it possesses jurisdiction over the subject matter, the parties or interests properly before it, and the governing instruments whose legal effect is sought to be determined.
The controversy presented concerns the legal interpretation of recorded conveyances affecting land designated by the Mathias El Tribe as MET-TL-SM-001. The authenticated record establishes that the governing instruments have been presented for judicial construction and that an actual controversy exists regarding their legal effect.
Because the controversy concerns the interpretation and legal consequences of those governing conveyances, the Court concludes that jurisdiction exists to determine the questions presented and to enter such declaratory and final relief as is authorized by governing law.
 
III. Authority
The authority exercised by this Court is not self-created, nor does it arise from mere assertion. Judicial authority exists only to the extent conferred by the governing law and must be exercised within the limits established by that law.
The Court's responsibility is not to create rights or obligations, but to identify, interpret, and apply the governing law to the facts established by the authenticated record. Every exercise of judicial power must therefore be traceable to a lawful source capable of supporting the action taken.
Accordingly, before determining the legal effect of any conveyance, recorded instrument, or governmental act, the Court must first identify the governing law from which the asserted authority derives. Where no lawful source exists, no exercise of authority may be sustained.
The Court therefore proceeds upon the principle that authority is demonstrated through governing law, not presumed through practice, custom, or administrative convenience. Judicial legitimacy depends upon faithful adherence to that principle.
 
IV. Judicial Function
The judicial function is one of interpretation, application, and judgment.
It is not the function of this Court to legislate, negotiate, administer governmental policy, or substitute its preferences for those expressed in the governing law. Rather, the Court is charged with resolving actual controversies by determining the legal effect of the governing instruments presented through the authenticated record.
In performing that duty, the Court follows a disciplined method of review:
1.	Identify the governing law applicable to the controversy.
2.	Determine the scope of authority conferred by that law.
3.	Establish the relevant facts from the authenticated record.
4.	Apply the governing law to those established facts.
5.	Enter the judgment required by law.
This sequence preserves the distinction between legislative authority, executive administration, and judicial interpretation, ensuring that each governmental function remains within its proper sphere.
The Court therefore declines to enlarge, diminish, or disregard the legal effect of governing instruments except where authorized by the governing law itself.
 
V. Questions Presented
The questions presented for determination are limited to those necessary to resolve the controversy before the Court.
The Court therefore considers the following questions:
1.	What are the governing conveyances and recorded instruments affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001?
2.	What governing law controls the interpretation of those instruments?
3.	What legal effect should be given to the authenticated conveyances according to the governing law?
4.	What respective rights, duties, interests, and obligations arise from those governing instruments?
5.	What declaratory and final relief is necessary to fully resolve the controversy presented by the authenticated record?
The Court expressly reserves all questions unnecessary to the disposition of this proceeding.
 
VI. Standard of Review
This matter presents questions concerning the interpretation and legal effect of governing conveyances and recorded instruments. Such questions are reviewed according to the governing law applicable to those instruments and are determined independently by the Court.
Recorded instruments are interpreted according to their text, the lawful authority under which they were executed, and the authenticated record presented to the Court. The objective of judicial review is to ascertain and give effect to the legal operation of the governing instruments rather than to rewrite, supplement, or disregard their expressed terms.
Where the governing law is clear, the Court applies that law according to its ordinary legal meaning. Where ambiguity exists, the Court construes the governing instruments in a manner that gives effect to the instrument as a whole, harmonizes its provisions where reasonably possible, and avoids interpretations that would render any material provision without legal effect.
The Court's review is confined to the issues necessary to resolve the controversy before it, and the judgment entered shall extend no further than required by the governing law and the authenticated record.

VIII. Conclusions of Law
Based upon the foregoing Findings of Fact, the governing law, the authenticated record, and the authorities properly before the Court, the Court reaches the following Conclusions of Law:
Conclusion No. 1 — Governing Law Controls
The legal effect of the governing conveyances affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001 must be determined according to the governing law under which those instruments were executed, acknowledged, recorded, and presented for judicial interpretation.
Neither administrative practice, unofficial custom, nor unilateral assertion may alter the legal operation of a governing instrument where the governing law prescribes its legal effect.
 
Conclusion No. 2 — Judicial Duty
Having determined that jurisdiction exists and that an actual controversy is properly before the Court, it is the duty of the Court to interpret the governing conveyances and declare their legal effect according to law.
The Court neither creates new property interests nor extinguishes existing rights. Rather, it declares the legal consequences that arise from the governing instruments when interpreted under the governing law.
 
Conclusion No. 3 — Construction of Governing Instruments
The governing conveyances and recorded instruments affecting MET-TL-SM-001 shall be construed as a unified body of related instruments where they concern the same property, transaction, trust relationship, or governmental purpose.
Each instrument shall be interpreted in harmony with the others whenever reasonably possible so that every operative provision is given legal effect and no material language is rendered meaningless through construction.
 
Conclusion No. 4 — Authority Must Be Demonstrated
Every governmental authority asserted with respect to the subject property must be supported by an identifiable source of governing law.
The Court concludes that authority affecting the legal rights established by the governing conveyances cannot rest solely upon administrative practice, convenience, assumption, or custom. Authority must instead be demonstrated by the governing law from which it is claimed to arise.
Accordingly, any asserted exercise of authority inconsistent with the governing law or contrary to the legal effect of the governing conveyances is without legal force to the extent of that inconsistency.
 
Conclusion No. 5 — Effect of Authenticated Conveyances
The Court concludes that duly executed, acknowledged, authenticated, and recorded conveyances possess legal effect according to the governing law applicable to those instruments.
The legal consequences arising from such conveyances are determined by law and not by subsequent disagreement concerning their existence, purpose, or effect.
Where the governing law recognizes the legal operation of a recorded instrument, the Court is bound to give that instrument the force and effect required by law.
 
Conclusion No. 6 — Scope of Judicial Relief
Because an actual controversy exists concerning the legal effect of the governing conveyances affecting MET-TL-SM-001, declaratory relief is appropriate to resolve uncertainty regarding the respective rights, duties, interests, and obligations arising from those instruments.
Final judgment shall therefore be entered declaring the legal effect of the governing conveyances to the extent necessary to resolve the controversy presented in this proceeding.
 
Conclusion No. 7 — Reservation of Unnecessary Questions
The Court declines to determine questions not necessary to the disposition of this matter.
Nothing in this Opinion shall be construed as deciding issues beyond those required to interpret the governing conveyances, determine their legal effect, and enter the declaratory and final relief set forth herein.

IX. Opinion of the Court
The controversy presented in this proceeding is neither abstract nor advisory. It arises from the legal effect of the governing conveyances and recorded instruments affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001. The Court's responsibility is therefore confined to determining the legal consequences that flow from those instruments under the governing law.
The Court begins with the principle that a recorded conveyance derives its legal effect from the governing law under which it was executed and recorded. The act of recording does not itself create rights; rather, recording serves to preserve, evidence, and provide legal notice of interests created or recognized through the governing instrument. Accordingly, judicial review must focus upon the legal operation of the conveyance itself rather than subsequent assumptions, administrative practices, or competing characterizations that are inconsistent with the governing law.
The authenticated record establishes that the governing conveyances affecting MET-TL-SM-001 were executed and recorded for identified legal purposes. Those instruments must therefore be read as a coherent whole. Where multiple recorded documents relate to the same property, transaction, trust relationship, or governmental purpose, they should be construed together so that each operative provision is given effect whenever reasonably possible.
The Court further concludes that the legal consequences of the governing conveyances cannot be altered by subsequent conduct that lacks authority under the governing law. Administrative action, private conduct, or governmental practice cannot enlarge, diminish, or negate the legal effect of a duly executed and recorded instrument unless the governing law itself authorizes such action. To hold otherwise would permit legal rights established through lawful conveyances to be modified by conduct independent of the authority from which those rights arise.
The Court therefore rejects any interpretation that would treat the governing conveyances as legally ineffective without first identifying a lawful basis for disregarding or modifying them. Judicial interpretation requires more than disagreement with the legal consequences of an instrument; it requires application of the governing law to the authenticated record. Where the governing law gives legal effect to an instrument, the Court is obligated to recognize that effect unless the instrument is shown to be invalid according to the same governing law.
Applying these principles to the record before it, the Court finds no basis to disregard the governing conveyances affecting MET-TL-SM-001. The authenticated record demonstrates the existence of the recorded instruments, and the Court has identified no controlling legal authority that renders those instruments void on their face or otherwise incapable of judicial enforcement. The Court therefore gives those conveyances the legal effect required by the governing law and resolves the controversy accordingly.
This determination is limited to the issues necessary to decide this proceeding. The Court does not undertake to resolve questions not presented by the record, nor does it issue advisory opinions concerning hypothetical disputes. The holdings that follow are confined to the interpretation and legal effect of the governing conveyances affecting MET-TL-SM-001 and the rights and obligations necessarily arising therefrom.
 
X. Declaratory Judgment

The Court declares that the governing conveyances and recorded instruments affecting Mathias El Tribe Land Designation No. MET-TL-SM-001 constitute the controlling instruments for determining the legal rights, interests, duties, and obligations addressed in this proceeding.
Those instruments shall be interpreted according to the governing law from which they derive their legal force and effect.
 
Declaration No. 2 — Unified Construction
The Court declares that the governing conveyances and related recorded instruments concerning MET-TL-SM-001 shall be construed together as a unified body of related instruments to the extent they concern the same property, transaction, trust relationship, or governmental purpose.
Each instrument shall be interpreted in a manner that gives effect to its operative provisions and harmonizes with the remaining governing instruments whenever reasonably possible.
 
Declaration No. 3 — Legal Effect
The Court declares that the legal effect of the governing conveyances shall be determined according to the governing law and the authenticated record.

Declaration No. 4 — Judicial Determination
The Court declares that the controversy presented concerning the interpretation and legal effect of the governing conveyances affecting MET-TL-SM-001 has been judicially determined to the extent set forth in this Opinion.
The declarations contained herein represent the Court's determination of the legal consequences arising from the governing instruments presented in this proceeding.
